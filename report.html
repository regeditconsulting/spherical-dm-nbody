Spherical N-body Simulations of Dark Matter Halos:
NFW Stability and SIDM Core Formation

Automated report from the spherical-dm-nbody pipeline
Abstract. We present a reproducible spherical N-body pipeline for dark matter halos based on the NSphere code. The phase-space is reduced from 6D to 3D \((r, v_r, \ell)\), enabling fast simulations of NFW halos with \(N \le 5\times10^4\) particles. We verify the numerical stability of the NFW profile over 10 dynamical times and demonstrate the formation of a flat core in the self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) model with cross-section \(\kappa = 100\,\mathrm{cm^2\,g^{-1}}\).

1. Introduction

The Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) profile is a standard description of cold dark matter (CDM) halos. Its central cusp is a robust prediction of collisionless N-body simulations. However, alternative models such as self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) predict core formation due to elastic scattering.

2. Methods

Initial conditions are generated with the built-in NSphere generator for an NFW profile with \(M_{\rm halo}=10^9\,M_\odot\), scale radius \(r_s=1\) kpc, and cutoff \(r_{\rm cut}=10\,r_s\). The gravitational evolution is followed using the adaptive leapfrog integrator.

4. Discussion

The NFW profile remains stable over 10 dynamical times, which validates the numerical setup. In the SIDM run, the central density is significantly reduced compared to the CDM case.
